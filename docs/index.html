01 · THE PROBLEM

The drug worked. The trial averaged it away.

A trial reports one number for everybody in it. A drug that helps a quarter of patients and does nothing for the rest can produce a flat number.

It gets shelved. Not for failing, but for being measured on the wrong people.

$79M

Median cost of an oncology trial measuring overall survival. Both failures in this deck were that kind of trial.

$41,413

Per patient enrolled. Every patient who cannot respond costs this much and dilutes your result.

43%

Of phase 3 oncology trials succeed. Most of that money buys a negative answer.

The field's own route to the same answer

2003 · FDA
Accelerated approval (subpart H), monotherapy after platinum and docetaxel failure, unselected NSCLC; based on IDEAL response rates
2005 · FDA
Label restricted to patients currently or previously benefiting; Iressa Access Program; after ISEL showed no OS benefit
2009 · EMA
Marketing authorisation restricted to EGFR-mutation-positive locally advanced/metastatic NSCLC
2012 · FDA
Sponsor voluntarily withdrew the NDA
2015 · FDA
Approval for first-line metastatic NSCLC with EGFR exon 19 deletion or L858R, with therascreen EGFR RGQ PCR companion diagnostic; based on IFUM (n=106, ORR 50%) supported by IPASS retrospective subset

12 years and a withdrawal to reach marker-selected patients. The evidence was there by 2009.

06 · WHEN IT SAYS NO

It also tells you not to bother.

Onartuzumab had the same evidence shape: a strong-looking marker-positive subgroup, from 66 patients. Its sponsor ran a 499-patient survival trial. Stopped for futility.

If that subgroup effect were real, what should the trial have returned? Using only pre-trial information:
